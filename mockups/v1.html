Educator Helper, redesigned for teachers

Three clickable directions for the same lesson: Alex Green’s Grade 6 class 6B, “Photosynthesis: how plants make food”. Same content and flow in each, so you can compare structure and feel rather than data.

  1. PrepareUpload or describe; the class sets grade and length
  2. CheckFix only what the AI was unsure about
  3. TeachRun it live or assign it to the class
  4. ResultsSee who needs help and act on it

Three directions

A. Planbook

Calm and careful. The app looks like the teacher’s own week planner, and checking the AI works like marking with a highlighter.

Home
The teaching week as a grid of periods. Today’s lesson shows “2 to check”; empty periods are one click from a new lesson.
Signature
The quiz reads like a document. Unsure lines are highlighted, with margin notes beside them like a colleague’s comments.
Best for
Teachers who prepare ahead and want to read everything before class.
Type and colour
Literata (bookish serif) with Figtree; ink blue, marking green, highlighter yellow for flags only.

B. Copilot

Fast and conversational. The teacher says what they need in one sentence, and every change the AI makes can be undone.

Home
“What are you teaching next?” in one prompt bar, with the next lesson above it.
Signature
The prompt bar turns a sentence into editable chips (class 6B, Grade 6, 45 min, Thu 14 Nov, the PDF). In Check, a thread lists flags and AI edits, each with Undo.
Best for
Teachers comfortable with chat assistants who like to ask rather than hunt for buttons.
Type and colour
Bricolage Grotesque with Atkinson Hyperlegible (made for low-vision readers); chalkboard green shell, chalk yellow for the main action only.

C. Class Pulse

Practical and glanceable. Everything is organised around the class period: before class, in class, after class.

Home
Today’s timetable with a “now” line. Period 3 with 6B shows the kit status and one button.
Signature
A period bar on every lesson screen with a live countdown, and a student-by-question grid that shows at a glance that Q4 is the problem.
Best for
Teaching live on a board or tablet, and teachers who want to act on results the same day.
Type and colour
Lexend (designed for reading fluency); cobalt for actions, green and red only for right and wrong, always with icons.

Recommendation

Build on A. Planbook as the frame, because the week planner matches how teachers already think and its check screen is the clearest answer to “hard to review AI output”. Borrow one idea from each of the other two:

  • From C: the before / in / after class stages and the student-by-question grid on Results.
  • From B: the “Ask for a change” bar inside Check, so a teacher can type “make Q3 easier” instead of finding a button.

All three use only patterns available in shadcn/ui (tabs, sheet, command, table, toggle, switch), so the chosen direction maps directly to the planned Next.js stack.
